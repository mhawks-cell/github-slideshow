Most troubled projects don't fail overnight — they erode. A missed milestone here, a scope change there, and before long your team is working nights and weekends with no end in sight. Knowing the early warning signs can save your initiative before it's too late.

## 1. Scope Has Grown But the Timeline Hasn't

If your project has absorbed new requirements without a corresponding adjustment to schedule or resources, you're carrying hidden risk. Every undocumented scope addition is a future delay waiting to happen.

**What to do:** Convene a scope review with all key stakeholders. Document every addition since baseline, quantify the impact, and negotiate a realistic revised plan.

## 2. Status Reports Feel Like Fiction

When project updates are full of "on track" and "green" but the team is visibly stressed, something is being masked. Happy-path reporting is a cultural symptom — often driven by fear of delivering bad news.

**What to do:** Shift from status theater to outcome-based reporting. Focus on what's been delivered, what's blocked, and what decisions are needed.

## 3. Key Stakeholders Have Gone Quiet

Disengagement is often a sign that confidence has eroded. When sponsors stop attending reviews or stop asking questions, they may have already mentally written off the project.

**What to do:** Schedule 1-on-1 conversations with disengaged stakeholders. Understand their concerns and re-establish a credible path forward.

## 4. The Team is Running on Fumes

Sustained crunch is a leading indicator of quality problems, turnover, and eventual delivery failure. A tired team makes mistakes.

**What to do:** Conduct an honest capacity assessment. If your team is over-committed, something has to give — scope, timeline, or resources. Pick one.

## 5. Nobody Agrees on What "Done" Looks Like

Unclear or conflicting definitions of success are the most common root cause of project failure. If your stakeholders have different mental models of the end state, conflict is inevitable.

**What to do:** Run a Definition of Done workshop. Get explicit written agreement on acceptance criteria before another sprint or phase begins.
